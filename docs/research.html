Her approach

Useful answers begin with honest approximations

Much of computational physics begins with an impossible request: follow every relevant degree of freedom, at every scale, without losing the structure of the underlying physics. Sara’s work asks where that description can be simplified—and what must be preserved for the result to remain meaningful.

This has led her from mixed quantum-classical dynamics and nuclear quantum effects to non-equilibrium statistical mechanics, rare events, electrochemical interfaces, and constrained molecular dynamics. The systems change; the discipline of connecting method, observable, and physical interpretation does not.

Approximation is not a shortcut around physics; it is a precise statement about which parts of the problem carry the answer.

Questions in motion

Three recurring lines of enquiry

01

How much quantum behaviour can a practical simulation retain?

Trajectory-based approximations, quantum thermal baths, and correlation functions connect formal quantum descriptions with realistic condensed-phase systems.

02

Can a constraint become a computational advantage?

Mass-Zero constrained dynamics—MaZe—handles fast electronic or polarization variables without hiding the physical assumptions that make the method work.

03

What happens at a driven molecular interface?

Statistical mechanics and molecular dynamics reveal organisation, transport, rare events, and phase behaviour at charged interfaces and away from equilibrium.

A common thread

Preserve the physics that carries the answer.

A faster calculation is useful only when its approximations are visible, testable, and appropriate to the question being asked.
